# BLACK VEIL - Story Bible

## 1. Premise

The year is 2027. The great powers spent the 2020s learning that data is more
valuable than territory, and that the fastest way to control a government is not
to invade it but to know what it has done. Into that vacuum stepped a private
network of former intelligence officers calling themselves **UMBRA**.

UMBRA does not sell secrets. UMBRA collects them, files them, and holds them. Its
ledger contains 214 senior officials across 14 countries, each entry annotated
with the price they accepted. Nobody has ever seen the whole ledger. It is the
most dangerous document on earth, and it is about to be transmitted.

The agency that should have stopped it - ARES, a deniable directorate buried inside
a Western intelligence service - has been compromised from the top for eleven
years. The man who compromised it is the same man who built UMBRA.

## 2. Protagonist - Marcus Vael (callsign VEIL)

- **Age:** 46. **Background:** former JSOC operator, then twelve years in a
  deniable action branch. Fourteen confirmed field operations.
- **Character:** quiet, precise, uncomfortable with authority but loyal to
  individuals. Speaks rarely and only when it changes something.
- **The wound:** Operation BRIGHT HARBOR, Beirut, 2021. Vael's team was burned by
  a leak from inside ARES. Six operators died. Vael survived because he was
  pulled out early - by an order he never saw and never forgave. He has spent
  five years assuming the leak was a mistake. Mission 10 proves it was policy.
- **Arc:** from instrument to author. The campaign is about whether he is still a
  tool being aimed by someone else. The finale is the first decision in years
  that is entirely his own.
- **Voice:** clipped, dry, almost never raises his voice. Uses first names, not
  ranks. Says "understood" instead of "yes sir".

## 3. Antagonist - Director Aldric Sorn

- **Age:** 58. **Position:** former Deputy Director of ARES; founder of UMBRA.
- **Motive (the important part):** Sorn is not a nihilist and not a profiteer. He
  spent twenty years watching elected governments leak operations, burn assets
  and lose wars because oversight bodies could not keep secrets. He concluded
  that democratic accountability of intelligence is not a virtue but a
  structural weakness - and that the only way to make the world stable is to
  make the people who decide wars personally accountable to someone who cannot
  be voted out. That someone is him.
- **Method:** he does not assassinate. He files. He prefers a minister who knows
  he is owned to a minister who is dead, because the living one votes.
- **He is not lying.** Everything he says about the failures of oversight is
  true. He is wrong about the conclusion, and he knows Vael will see that.
- **Relationship to Vael:** Sorn recruited him. Sorn signed the order that pulled
  him out of Beirut - not to save him, but because a surviving witness to a
  burned operation is a useful piece on the board.
- **Voice:** calm, articulate, never threatens. Asks questions he already knows
  the answers to. Believes he is being reasonable.

## 4. Supporting cast

**Zara Mehta - Handler.** 41. ARES senior analyst, ran the network that survived
when Beirut collapsed. Pragmatic to the point of ruthlessness, and the only
person who ever told Vael the truth about Beirut. She is the campaign's moral
compass and its most compromised character: she authorised three of the
operations UMBRA later used as leverage.

**Leon Daye - Field Technician.** 33. Ex-signals, brilliant, talks constantly
because silence makes him nervous. Handles interception, comms and remote
support. Provides the humour that keeps the tone from becoming oppressive.

**Yuki Tanaka - Callsign KESTREL.** 36. Deep-cover asset inside UMBRA for four
years, running as an internal security analyst. Recruited by Sorn personally,
which means she has done things for UMBRA she cannot undo. She is the campaign's
unresolved question: asset, collaborator, or both. Missions 7, 11 and 12 pay
off a thread planted in mission 5.

**General Aris Vasic - UMBRA military arm.** 60. Runs UMBRA's enforcement and
the BROKEN ARROW program. Believes in Sorn's project with the fervour of a man
who has never had to justify a war to a parliament. Antagonist of missions 4, 7
and 10.

## 5. Organisation and world

**ARES** - deniable action directorate. Funded from a black budget line that does
not appear in any published estimate. Officially does not exist. Provides Vael's
support, and is riddled with UMBRA penetration.

**UMBRA** - private intelligence network. 300 personnel, nine field stations, one
operations centre in Prague, and Sorn's estate on the Black Sea coast. Its
funding comes from its clients: governments that pay to keep their own secrets
buried, and, increasingly, to have someone else's secrets delivered.

**The Ledger** - 214 names, 41,000 pages of supporting material. Sorn's endgame
is not to publish it but to transmit it: to hand each government the specific
file on its own officials, thereby making every one of them permanently
cooperative. Once transmitted, the compromise is mutual and irreversible.

## 6. Campaign arc

**Act I - The thread (Missions 1-4).**
A dead informant and a false ledger lead Vael to a shipping network, a power
utility on the take, and a corporate archive that does not exist on any floor
plan. The trail is deliberate: Sorn is not hiding, he is recruiting. Each mission
is a job interview Vael does not know he is attending.

**Act II - The structure (Missions 5-8).**
The Directorate's own archive reveals the black budget that created ARES and the
name that signed it. The name is not in the personnel directory because it was
removed in 2016. Vael learns that UMBRA's reach includes a serving minister, an
arms broker and a general with a directed-energy weapon program. KESTREL goes
dark. The campaign's central question becomes: who is KESTREL, really?

**Act III - The reckoning (Missions 9-12).**
The exchange, the weapon, and the operations centre fall in sequence. KESTREL's
identity resolves - and it is not clean. Vael reaches the estate with the ledger
in reach, the transmission eleven hours out, and three ways to end it: destroy
the ledger (nobody is exposed, nobody is freed), transmit it publicly (everyone
is exposed, including ARES's own crimes), or trade it to Sorn for KESTREL's life.

## 7. Endings

| Choice | Ending |
|--------|--------|
| Public transmission | "Daylight". Fourteen governments convulse. ARES is dissolved. Vael is exposed as the source and disappears. Zara testifies. The world is worse for six months and better for twenty years. |
| Destroy the ledger | "Ash". Nobody is exposed, nobody is freed. UMBRA's network survives without its master. Vael is promoted into the institution he just proved was rotten. Coldest ending. |
| Trade for KESTREL | "Debt". Sorn walks with a copy. KESTREL lives. Vael spends the epilogue hunting a man who now owns him. |

## 8. Tone and presentation rules

- **No exposition during gameplay.** All story arrives through radio check-ins
  under 25 words, environmental documents, and behaviour.
- **Show consequences physically.** A mission that went loud leaves bullet damage
  and extra guards in the next level's shared assets where possible.
- **Sorn is never cartoonish.** He is reasonable in every scene, which is what
  makes him frightening.
- **Vael does not monologue.** His arc is carried by what he chooses to do when
  nobody is asking him to do anything.

## 9. Intel as narrative delivery

Every mission contains three collectible items that develop the story outside the
radio channel: two documents (policy, logistics, evidence) and one audio log
(personal, human, often incriminating). The audio logs carry the emotional arc:
mission 1 is the informant's last message, mission 5 is KESTREL's first, mission
11 is KESTREL's confession, mission 12 is Zara's farewell. Reading all 36 items
reconstructs the full history of ARES and UMBRA, including the parts Vael is not
told.
